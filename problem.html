PROBLEM OVERVIEW

Problem Statement

Development of a Low Power, Real-Time Adaptive Software-Defined Sonar Transmitter Payload for Autonomous Underwater Vehicles (AUVs).

Problem Description

Autonomous Underwater Vehicles (AUVs) require intelligent, energy-efficient sonar payloads that can dynamically adapt acoustic frequencies and transmission profiles across changing subsea water columns without manual intervention.

THE SUBSEA DILEMMA

Turbidity vs Spatial Resolution

Clear Shallow Reef

500 kHz Required
• Requirement: High acoustic frequency (500 kHz) to achieve high spatial mapping resolution.
• Traditional Sonar: Stuck at fixed 250 kHz compromise ➔ loses fine obstacle details.
• S.A.L.S.A Response: Automatically switches to 500 kHz with LFM Up Chirp.

Muddy Sediment Estuary

100 kHz Required
• Requirement: Low acoustic frequency (100 kHz) to penetrate heavy suspended sediment.
• Traditional Sonar: High attenuation at 250 kHz ➔ total signal blindness.
• S.A.L.S.A Response: Automatically switches to 100 kHz Phase-Coded mode.
CRITICAL LIMITATIONS

5 Key Sonar Bottlenecks Solved

🔒

1. Fixed Frequency

Single factory-set frequency cannot adapt as AUV transits through changing water.

🌊

2. Water Variability

Murkiness causes acoustic scattering while clear water requires higher resolution.

🔋

3. High Power Drain

Continuous full-power broadcast cuts AUV onboard battery runtime by 50%.

👤

4. Lack of Autonomy

Changing sonar settings traditionally requires human intervention mid-mission.

📉

5. Signal Degradation & Noise

Lack of multi-stage analog filtering causes high harmonic distortion and poor signal-to-noise ratio.
